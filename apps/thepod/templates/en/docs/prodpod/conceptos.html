CONCEPTS

The prodPod vocabulary

The key terms you need to know to record in prodPod.

01 Session

A recording session. It moves through these states:

  1. Preparing: it's being set up, participants haven't joined yet.
  2. Live: participants are connected to the studio.
  3. Recording: recording is active in every browser.
  4. Finished: recording has ended, the tracks are ready.
  5. Archived: the session was archived after being sent to postPod.

02 Participant

Someone connected to the session. There are three roles:

  • Host: controls the session (starts/stops recording, soundboard, streaming, invitations).
  • Co-host: can assist the host and has access to the backstage chat.
  • Guest: takes part in the recording without admin controls.

Guests get a special link to join without needing a thePod account.

03 Local recording

Each participant records their own audio and video directly in their browser. This means the recording quality doesn't depend on internet speed. Even if the video call looks laggy or pixelated, the local recording stays clean and in high quality.

While you record, the chunks are uploaded to the server in the background — there's no long upload wait when you finish, and if the browser closes by accident, whatever was already uploaded isn't lost.

TIP

If you briefly lose your connection, your local recording keeps running. Once you reconnect, you'll see everyone else again.

04 Mic check (microphone level)

An assistant that listens to your microphone for 10 seconds, measures the level and suggests an optimal gain. The goal is for you to speak at a comfortable, even volume. Your settings are remembered for that specific microphone — if you always use the same one, you only need to do it once.

05 Soundboard

A panel of pads with sounds and effects. You set it up in prePod and it arrives in the session automatically. When the host fires a sound, every participant hears it. Triggers are logged with their exact time so postPod can place them on the timeline.

CUE

Alt+click on a pad plays it only in your headphones (it isn't broadcast). Handy for checking a sound before firing it live.

06 Chapter markers

Timestamps you drop while recording to flag chapters or important sections. There are two kinds:

  • Planned: they come from the prePod rundown. They show up in a list so you can activate them when you reach that point.
  • Live: you create them on the fly while recording.

Both become moments when the recording reaches postPod.

07 Chat

Two communication channels:

  • Studio chat: visible to every participant.
  • Backstage: visible only to the host and co-hosts. Perfect for coordinating (“let's wrap up”, “skip this topic”) without guests seeing it.

Messages are saved in the session history.

08 Countdown

A synchronized 3-second count that appears on every participant's screen. When it hits zero, recording starts simultaneously in every browser, making sure there's no offset between the tracks.

09 Screen sharing

One participant at a time can share their screen, a window or a tab. The shared screen is recorded as a separate track.

10 RTMP streaming

Live streaming to YouTube, Twitch or any custom RTMP server. It runs alongside the local recording: you get both the high-quality files and the live broadcast at the same time.

11 Practice mode

A rehearsal mode that works exactly like a real session but without recording. Use it to test audio levels, practice with the soundboard, check screen sharing and make sure everything works before the real take.

12 Tracks

The resulting recordings: one audio file and one video file per participant, plus the screen-share recordings. All tracks are synchronized and go to postPod as separate files so you can edit with precision.
