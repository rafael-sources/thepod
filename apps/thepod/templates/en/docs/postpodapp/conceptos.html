CONCEPTS

How the app thinks

A few ideas, well defined. If you get these, you get everything the app does.

01 Project

Every episode you bring in is a project with its own folder inside Movies → postPod: that's where the videos, sound effects, sponsors, branding and everything you export live. Deleting a project from the app sends it to the Trash.

02 Tracks and people

Each recorded person is a track with their video and audio. Tracks can start at different times (someone who joined late): the app respects that offset so everything stays in sync. The app knows who's talking by measuring the volume of each track, and uses that to switch cameras in the automatic layouts.

03 Non-destructive cuts

Just like in postPod web, nothing is ever really deleted. The silences, filler words and sentences you remove are stored as cuts over the original recording time. The preview and the export skip those stretches. You can undo them whenever you want. Muting one person for a stretch (a cough, a bump) isn't a cut: the video doesn't get shorter, only their volume drops there (see Mix).

Type of cutWhere it comes from
SilenceLong pauses where nobody talks (any stretch with audible voice is protected)
Filler word“uh”, “umm”, “mmm”… and, if you turn it on, “este”, “o sea”, “pues”, “bueno”
SentenceThe ones you cut by hand in Subtitles
ImportedThe cuts you already made in the postPod web transcript
StretchWhat you mark by dragging in the timeline and cut “from everyone”
Reel onlyThe scissors in a reel's text: removed from that reel, not from the episode

04 Transcription

The app transcribes each track separately, on your Mac and without internet, noting the exact time of every word. Then it merges the tracks and discards the duplicate words that leak from one microphone into another. Subtitles, filler-word cuts and silence detection all come from that transcript.

05 Moments and reels

Moments are the ones you mark in postPod web (the M key) or live in prodPod. The app doesn't invent reels: each moment becomes a reel, with the same rule the web uses (the moment's length, or up to the next moment). You can also add a moment from the app (Mark moment, or the M, I and O keys). Each reel can have its own layout, subtitles, music, b-roll and lower thirds, separate from the episode. Whatever lies outside the reel's stretch doesn't go in it.

06 Formats and layouts

The format is the size of the video; the layout is how the cameras are arranged inside it.

LayoutWhat it does
AutomaticPicks the best one for the format
SpeakerFull screen for whoever is talking
Side by sideEveryone at once, next to each other
GridEveryone in a grid
LeadThe speaker large, the rest along the side
Picture in pictureThe speaker large, someone else in a corner
Top and bottomOne on top and one below (ideal for vertical)
StudioThe cameras in frames over your podcast background

On top of the layout go, in this order: the video (or the b-roll), the lower thirds, the layers (texts, stickers, images) and, above everything, the subtitles.

07 Layers and b-roll

A layer is something that shows over the video for a while: a text, a sticker or an image. It has a start, a duration and an in and out animation, and you move it by dragging it over the preview. B-roll is a video or a photo that covers the screen for a few seconds (or goes in a small window) while the voice keeps going; if it has sound, it's mixed in; if it's muted, it's only seen. If you cut the stretch where a layer started, it shows up in whatever comes next.

08 Lower thirds

The news-style texts that slide in at the bottom: title, text and tag (NOW, SEGMENT, SPONSORED BY…), with the podcast or sponsor logo, animation and a short sound. Three styles: News, Clean and Sponsor. The ones the director launched live from prodPod arrive already placed at their second, marked “from the director”, and are edited the same way.

09 Mix and drawn volume

Each person has their waveform, a meter, S (solo) and M (mute), and a yellow line of volume you draw with points: between points the volume changes gradually. What you draw is added to the automatic stuff (fades, muting whoever isn't talking, bleeps), and yours wins. On export, the final loudness is measured and brought to −16 LUFS, which is what Spotify and YouTube ask for. If you want to finish the mix in another program, Separate tracks gives you one WAV per person and another with music and effects.

10 Sensitive words

Swear words and topics that social networks down-rank (death, drugs, sex, weapons…), found in the transcript. For each one you decide: bleep on top (covers the word on every microphone, the original audio is untouched), change the subtitle to the euphemism used on social media (“unalive”, “substances”…), both, or leave it as is. Swear words come with a bleep and a softened subtitle; sensitive topics only change the subtitle.

11 Teleprompter

A section of the prePod script marked as teleprompter: it says who reads it (from the list of hosts and guests), when it appears (minutes and seconds from the start of the recording, or after the previous one with a gap), the speed and how much of the screen it takes. In the prod room it fires on its own: everyone sees the heads-up 3 s before and the text shows only to the reader. The director can also launch one by hand from the control room.

12 Reel templates

The reel design (camera layout, branding, subtitles, order) is saved under a name and applied with one click in any episode. It doesn't save your custom logo or background, because those are files that belong to each episode.
