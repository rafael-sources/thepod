FEATURES

Every tool in detail

Everything you can do in prePod, explained step by step.

1 Branding

The branding editor has four main sections:

  • Identity: podcast name, tagline (a short phrase) and long description.
  • Colors: five color pickers with hex input (primary, secondary, accent, background and text).
  • Typography: heading font, body text font and alignment.
  • Assets: upload the logo (up to 500x500 pixels) and the cover art (up to 1200x800 pixels).

In the right column there's a live preview that reflects every change instantly.

Hosts: below the identity, you can add co-hosts by typing their thePod username. The podcast owner is marked with a crown.

TIP

Changes are saved automatically after 60 seconds of inactivity. You can also save manually with the "Save" button.

2 Episode board (Kanban)

Six columns that represent the production flow:

  1. Idea: episodes at the concept stage.
  2. Planning: in active preparation.
  3. With script: rundown and script completed.
  4. Recorded: the recording is done.
  5. Published: episode published and distributed.
  6. Archived: closed episodes that leave the day-to-day view (column hidden by default).

Each card shows the number (EP #), title, scheduled date and estimated duration.

Available actions:

  • Drag cards between columns to change the status.
  • Click a card to open its detail view.
  • Use the arrows (up/down) to change the order on the public landing page.
  • Click + New episode in any column to create one directly in that state.

3 Episode detail

When you open an episode, you'll find:

  • Header: status (dropdown), episode number (editable) and title (editable inline).
  • Description: a free-form text area to describe the episode.
  • Metadata: status picker, date and time, estimated duration in minutes.
  • Download .ics: export this episode as a calendar event.
  • Show notes: appear automatically when the episode moves to "Published".
  • Integration bar: shows the production status and the buttons to send to prodPod or postPod.

4 Rundown

The rundown editor lets you build the episode structure with blocks. Each block has:

  • Type: Intro, Segment, Interview, Sponsor, Break, Outro or Other.
  • Title: a descriptive name for the block.
  • Estimated duration: in minutes.
  • Notes: cues or reference content.
  • Sponsor (Sponsor blocks only): a picker to link a registered advertiser.

Drag the six-dot handle to reorder blocks. The total estimated duration is shown at the bottom. Rundown blocks become chapter markers when you send the episode to prodPod, and show notes when the episode is published.

5 Script

The script editor is more detailed than the rundown. Each section has:

  • Speaker: the name of who's talking.
  • Content: the text or guide for what's said.
  • Notes: technical or production cues.
  • Duration: estimated time in seconds.

Use the up/down arrows to reorder sections. The script saves automatically every time you stop typing.

6 Guests (Roster)

To add a guest:

  1. Click "Add guest".
  2. Fill in name, email (optional) and role (Host, Co-host or Guest).
  3. Optionally, add a bio, a photo and their social links (X/Twitter, Instagram, YouTube, TikTok).

The photo and social links travel with the episode: prodPod and postPod use them for banners and credits.

RSVP system: to confirm attendance you have two paths:

  • Invite by email: click "Invite" and an automatic email is sent with the confirmation link.
  • Copy link: if there's no email available, copy the RSVP link and send it manually.

The status updates automatically: Pending, Yes, No or Maybe. Guests don't need a thePod account.

7 Ideas and voting

The Ideas side panel lets you capture quick concepts for future episodes:

  • Write an idea (up to 500 characters) and submit it with Ctrl+Enter.
  • Each team member can vote once per idea.
  • Accept or reject ideas with the corresponding buttons.
  • Accepted ideas can be turned into episodes.

8 Calendar

The Calendar tab shows a monthly calendar with all your episodes:

  • Move between months with the arrows or jump back to today.
  • Click an empty day: creates a new episode on that date.
  • Click an episode: opens its detail view.
  • Drag an episode to another day: reschedules the date.
  • Download .ics: exports the whole calendar to import into your calendar app.

9 Sounds (Soundboard)

The Sounds tab manages your library of audio effects:

  • Upload sounds with the "Upload sound" button.
  • Each sound has an editable name, a keyboard shortcut (a single key) and a custom color.
  • Play each sound with the play button.
  • Access a global library of sounds shared across all your podcasts.

When you send an episode to prodPod, your full soundboard goes along so you can use it during the live recording.

10 Templates

Templates speed up creating episodes with a recurring format:

  • Create: click "+ New template" and give it a name.
  • Edit: each template has a description, a base rundown and a base script.
  • Use: when creating a new episode, select the template to pre-fill the structure.
  • Default: mark a template as the default so it's applied automatically to every new episode.

11 Sponsors and reports

The Sponsors panel lets you manage advertisers:

  • Create sponsor: name, URL, ad-read text, notes.
  • Status: active or paused (toggle).
  • Link: in the rundown, "Sponsor" blocks show a picker to choose the advertiser.
  • Report: total episodes, blocks, estimated minutes and a detailed table per episode.
  • Export CSV: download a file with the full detail to share with the advertiser.

12 Show notes

Generated automatically when an episode moves to the Published state. They contain the title, chapters derived from the rundown blocks with YouTube-compatible timestamps, and credits.

Two quick-copy buttons:

  • Copy all: title + chapters + credits.
  • Chapters only: YouTube format, ready to paste.

13 Public landing page + RSS

The Landing tab creates a web page for your podcast:

  • Design: choose from three templates (Minimal, Bold, Classic).
  • RSS feed: generated automatically. Compatible with Apple Podcasts, Spotify and other platforms.
  • Platform links: add your podcast's URLs on YouTube, Spotify, Apple Podcasts, Instagram, TikTok and X/Twitter.
  • Team: add members with avatar, name, role, bio and social links.
  • Custom content: visual (WYSIWYG) editor with headings, paragraphs, lists, quotes and images.
  • Advanced customization: fields for custom CSS and analytics code.

Includes a preview with a reload button so you can see changes before publishing.

14 Distribution links

In each episode's detail view, the links section lets you record the URLs where it was published. Available platforms: YouTube, Spotify, Apple Podcasts, Google Podcasts, Podbean, Overcast, Castbox, iHeart and your own site. These links can be shown on your public landing page.

15 Publishing the episode

In the episode detail view, Publish section: pick the final MP3 or M4A from your computer and tap Upload and publish. It uploads in chunks with a progress bar, the episode moves to Published and the feed updates on its own, ready for Spotify and Apple Podcasts to pick it up. If you picked the wrong file, “Replace audio” swaps it out.

16 Distribution panel

Tools to get your RSS feed into the podcast directories:

  • Feed validation: checks that your RSS meets Apple/Spotify requirements before you submit it.
  • Status per platform: record which directories you've already submitted the podcast to (Spotify, Apple Podcasts, YouTube, etc.) and their status.
  • Automatic check: verifies that the feed responds correctly from the outside.

17 Upload to YouTube

Connect the podcast's YouTube channel once (Connect YouTube button in the episode's Publish section). Then pick the video, adjust the title and whether it's Public, Unlisted or Private, and tap Upload to YouTube: you'll see the upload progress and the video link appears when it's done. The description is built from the episode notes. You can disconnect the account whenever you want.

18 Social: connecting your accounts

In Podcast settings → Social you connect, just once, the accounts where the podcast gets published. It's only for connecting: publishing reels and episodes happens in pubPod, inside thePod for Mac. Each account lights up its stars there:

  • Podcast feed: Spotify and Apple Podcasts. Ready to go once the podcast is set up for publishing.
  • YouTube: YouTube and Shorts. It's the same connection as the YouTube upload.
  • Instagram: Instagram and Reels. Needs a professional account (Creator or Business); while thePod is under Meta review, only accounts added as testers.
  • TikTok: reels land in your TikTok inbox, with the text ready, and you publish them from your phone.
  • Facebook: coming soon.

Connect opens the network to grant permission and comes back to prePod on its own; you'll see the connected user and Disconnect removes the permission. The only permission you grant is to publish on your behalf; passwords are never stored. What thePod publishes can be reviewed in Privacy.

19 API for the desktop app (Mac)

prePod exposes a chunked publishing API designed for the macOS desktop app: large file uploads in chunks, audio publishing to hosting + RSS, video upload to YouTube and social publishing (pubPod: Instagram, Shorts, TikTok, right away or scheduled), with progress polling. If you only use the browser, there's nothing to set up.
