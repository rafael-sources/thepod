CONCEPTS

The postPod vocabulary

The key terms you need to know to edit in postPod. If you're coming from another video editor, they'll sound familiar.

01 Project

Your editing workspace. It holds all the media files, the timeline, the moments and the export history. Each project is independent. If your project comes from prodPod, it already includes the recordings and chapter markers.

02 Files (media files)

The video, audio and image files in your project. You upload them manually or they arrive from prodPod. The original files are never modified — they're the untouched source your edits are built on.

Supported formats: MP4, MOV, WebM (video), MP3, WAV, M4A (audio), JPG, PNG, GIF (image). Maximum size per file: 2 GB.

03 Timeline and clips

The timeline is the sequence of clips that makes up your final result. A clip is a reference to a media file (or a portion of it) placed at a specific position. You can reorder, trim and delete clips without touching the original file.

Clips can live on different tracks (Track 1, Track 2) for multi-camera workflows, letting you switch between camera angles while editing.

04 Audio lane

A parallel audio track below the main timeline, ideal for background music, ambience or intro/outro stingers. It plays along with the main content. Each clip in the audio lane has its own volume control and start/end points.

05 Moments

Timestamps you place on the timeline to anchor operations. A moment can be:

  • Relative (default): it moves if you add or remove clips before it. This is the most useful type in most cases.
  • Absolute: it stays at a fixed time no matter what changes in the timeline.

You can give a moment a duration to define a range (start to end) where its operations apply. If your project came from prodPod, the recording's chapter markers already show up as moments.

06 Operations

Effects and transformations applied at a moment. You can add multiple operations to the same moment.

07 Selected range

A yellow band on the timeline that you create by dragging across the ruler. It's used for:

  • Partial export: export only that portion of the video (ideal for social media clips).
  • Preview loop: loop that section to review the effects before exporting.
  • Timeline zoom: press Z to zoom into the selection and edit with more precision.

08 Transcript cuts

Edits made in the Transcript Editor. When you delete text from the transcript, the matching audio/video is cut from the export. It's the most intuitive way to remove filler words, long pauses or sections you don't want. All cuts are non-destructive and reversible.

09 Captions

Word-by-word subtitles generated by Whisper (an AI model). Available in multiple languages. They can be shown live during playback, burned into the exported video, or exported as standalone SRT files.

10 Export jobs

Every export is a background task. You pick a preset, optionally turn on options (burn captions, audio enhancement, generate notes, partial range), and the job shows its progress until it completes. You can download the result or play it right away.

12 Non-destructive editing

The core principle of postPod. Your original files stay untouched. Every cut, operation and adjustment is saved as instructions in the project. When you export, all the instructions are applied to generate the final file.

  • You can change, remove or add operations at any time without losing anything.
  • You can export the same project several times with different presets.
  • If you delete an operation, the effect disappears — there's nothing to restore.
